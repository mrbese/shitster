shitster.

Being angry and being precise are not mutually exclusive.

Somewhere in a contract you never negotiated, a company reserved the right to do the thing that just made you furious. The same contract says you cannot sue together, cannot go to a jury, and must argue alone, in a forum they picked, under rules they wrote and update whenever they like.

shitster builds small, free, open-source tools that turn one person's anger into the exact paperwork those same contracts already entitle them to. No accounts. No stored data. No fees. Nothing is sent on your behalf. The tool prepares, the human acts.

What we hold

Liability should not shrink as harm scales. When a contract makes it cheaper per person to wrong a million people than to wrong one, that clause is not procedure, it is a volume discount on wrongdoing.
The drafter of the rules cannot be their beneficiary. Terms that one side rewrites at will, with opt-out windows that expired before you knew they existed, are not agreements. They are weather.
Aggregation for me but not for thee is immunity, not procedure. The same documents that ban class actions mandate batching when it cuts the company's fees. Collective action is either legitimate or it is not. Pick one.

How it works

Everything runs in your browser. Documents are generated client-side and never touch a server. The code is public, the legal reasoning cites the actual contracts (committed to the repo as evidence), and every claim is reviewed adversarially before it ships. When we are wrong, the issue tracker is right there.

open source: github.com/mrbese/shitster. Nothing here is legal advice. These are self-help tools. You act in your own name, at your own judgment, for your own claim.
